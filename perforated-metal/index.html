What we perforate

Perforated metal is flat sheet punched with a regular pattern of holes. It is used wherever you need a combination of strength, airflow, light, sound absorption, screening or a distinctive appearance: machine guards, acoustic panels, ventilation grilles, balustrade infill, facades and sunscreens, sieves and filters, speaker grilles, radiator covers and shop fittings.

We stock perforated sheet in mild steel, pre-galvanised steel, stainless steel, aluminium and copper. Patterns follow the DIN 24041 convention: R for round holes, C for square holes and LR for slots, with T indicating a 60° staggered pitch and U a straight (square) pitch. So R5 T8 is a 5mm round hole on an 8mm staggered pitch. Our hole pattern guide explains the notation in full.

Stock sheet or bespoke?

Stock perforations are held in standard sheet sizes, typically 2000 × 1000mm and 2500 × 1250mm, and ship on a next-working-day basis. Because we perforate in-house we can also produce non-standard hole sizes, pitches and patterns, leave unperforated margins or areas for fixing, and supply cut-to-size, folded, rolled or coated panels. See fabrication and coating, or send us a drawing.

How to choose

Start from the job the sheet has to do. For screening and sieving, the hole size is the governing dimension. For guarding, the hole must be small enough to keep fingers and tools out at the working distance. For airflow and acoustics, open area matters most, and staggered round holes give the highest open area for a given hole size. For architectural work, appearance and material finish usually lead. The perforation finder lets you filter all stock patterns by material, hole shape, hole size, open area and thickness, and the open area and weight calculator covers anything not in the catalogue.

Perforated metal FAQs

What do the pattern codes like R5 T8 mean?

They follow DIN 24041. The first letter is the hole shape: R round, C square, LR slot. The number after it is the hole size in millimetres. The second letter is the pitch arrangement: T for a 60° staggered (triangular) pitch, U for a straight (square) pitch, followed by the centre-to-centre pitch in millimetres. R5 T8 is therefore a 5mm round hole on an 8mm staggered pitch. See the hole pattern guide.

What is open area and why does it matter?

Open area is the percentage of the sheet that is hole rather than metal. It governs airflow, light transmission, drainage rate and acoustic transparency, and also strength and weight: a 60% open sheet is much lighter and more flexible than a 25% one. Round staggered patterns give the highest open area for a given hole size. Calculate it for any pattern with the open area calculator.

What sheet sizes do you stock?

Most patterns are held in 2000 × 1000mm and/or 2500 × 1250mm sheets. Each product page shows exactly which sizes and thicknesses are in stock. We cut to size and can produce other sheet sizes to order.

Can you perforate a pattern that is not in the catalogue?

Yes. We perforate in-house and can produce specific hole sizes, pitches and patterns, unperforated margins and areas, and architectural or visual panels to your drawing. Send us the details for a quote.

Do you supply small quantities?

Yes. Single sheets and cut pieces are welcome alongside trade and production quantities.

What are margins on a perforated sheet?

Margins are the unperforated strips along the sheet edges. Stock sheet is supplied with small side margins and no end margins (the pattern runs off the sheet ends). For fabricated panels we can leave specified margins on any edge so you have solid metal for folding, welding or fixing.
